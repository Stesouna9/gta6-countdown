GTA 6: Guardian visit traces Vice City research trips back to 2015

Notebookcheck reports early details from the Guardian visit to Rockstar North, ahead of a fuller feature scheduled for November 14.

Vice City's development began with field research rather than a finished virtual street map. According to Notebookcheck, Guardian journalist Keza MacDonald says Rockstar's first research trips took place in 2015. She visited Rockstar North in Edinburgh and spoke with Rob Nelson and Jennifer Kolbe. Her initial impressions precede a fuller account scheduled for the Guardian's Saturday Magazine on November 14.

What did Rockstar study in Florida?

Notebookcheck's account describes Nelson's approach as beginning with the actual place: its history, atmosphere and everyday experiences. Those observations then help shape the story, characters and encounters. It is a useful distinction when discussing authenticity. The reported method concerns the foundations of the game world, rather than simply matching buildings to reference photographs.

The trips included clubs, bars in the Florida Keys and police ride-alongs through South Beach, according to the report. These examples describe research experiences. They do not establish that players will receive a mission based on each visit, nor do they reveal a specific route through the finished story. The preview remains an account of development work.

What did the demonstration show?

Notebookcheck says MacDonald describes Jason eating a donut while walking and drinking beer. When he starts running, he places a six-pack under his arm, with liquid visibly moving inside the bottles. The example concerns how an animation handles an object through a change of movement. It is a small observation with a concrete visual basis in the demonstration being reported.

Another anecdote concerns a jewelry store robbery. Notebookcheck recounts MacDonald's description of Nelson objecting to the position of a security guard's feet. That suggests close attention to background animation, but it is not a comprehensive assessment of the finished game. A specific correction during one demonstration cannot establish the quality of every scene or reveal additional gameplay systems.

Why it matters

The account connects Florida research to details that might otherwise pass unnoticed on screen. It builds on our reporting about Nelson's comments on GTA locations and the Guardian's history of the series. Neither that context nor this preview should be mistaken for a review of the finished release.

The complete feature is still ahead, so further revelations must wait for publication. Our news journal keeps the available reporting together. For now, the significant addition is a development perspective: the reported trips and animation anecdotes show how Rockstar connects a real place to the everyday behavior of its fictional residents.
