GTA 6 PC port: Take-Two confirms DMCA against Leonida, 3,007 repos

Take-Two confirmed it filed the DMCA notice that took down Leonida: GitHub disabled 3,007 repositories, while other GTA 6 PC port projects remain online.

Fonti tweaktown.com · frandroid.com · gamekult.com

Leonida's removal is no longer just a disappearance: Take-Two Interactive filed it. According to TweakTown, the publisher's DMCA notice, published by GitHub, targets NotRexed's repository and more than 2,900 forks, a network of 3,007 repositories disabled in one go.

What does Take-Two accuse Leonida of?

In its complaint, relayed by Frandroid, the publisher says Leonida reproduces protected elements of GTA 6 and is an unlawful derivative work. It also cites section 2.3 of Rockstar's terms of service, which bars decompiling, decrypting or reverse engineering its games.

The timeline is the sharpest point. Per the notice, stolen files from the Xbox Series build were distributed on November 20, and the repository opened the next day. The project told users to bring their own game files; Take-Two calls that instruction "intentionally misleading", since the only files available at the time were the leaked ones.

Are the other PC port projects affected?

No, according to both outlets: the notice names no other project. VIctory, started by developer Marvis, and XCL, an Xbox compatibility layer for Windows, are still online. TweakTown adds that a repository called Leonida Continued carries NotRexed's full commit history, but its README says the game "does not boot yet". VIctory reports loading the world and drawing the first mission's intro at one to two seconds per frame. Gamekult notes that Leonida's creator had said he was stepping away to focus on his studies.

What changed since our piece on Leonida's withdrawal: Take-Two's role is now documented, where before it rested on NotRexed's own message. For the leak behind it all, see also the Xbox executable published by Cyberleek.

Is an official PC version announced?

No. Rockstar has announced no PC version, and the images from unofficial projects remain dark, untextured and unlit, at a few frames per second at best. None of it is playable. Official release details stay on sortie.html.

Why it matters

The question is no longer whether Rockstar and Take-Two react, but how far they go. One project fell, the others keep moving, and legal action does not fix the underlying problem: the leaked build is still out there. Be careful, too, with fake "clients" and downloads that feed on curiosity about a PC port.
