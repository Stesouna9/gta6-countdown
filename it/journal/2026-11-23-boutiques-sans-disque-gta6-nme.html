GTA 6 no-disc release: UK game shops say they are barely selling it

UK game shop owners quoted by NME and the BBC say GTA 6, sold only digitally or as a code in a box, is bringing them almost no sales.

Fonti nme.com

GTA 6 is selling millions of copies, just not in neighbourhood shops. According to NME, citing the BBC, UK game store owners say they sell almost nothing because the game exists only as a digital download or a code in a box.

What are shop owners saying?

Richard Tewksbury, owner of Super Game Shack in Leicester, told the BBC his store has sold just four PS5 copies and one Xbox copy, "embarrassing" sales. His explanation: "No-one sadly wants to buy a code in a box." He adds that the game is breaking sales records but that has not translated for him. For game shops, he says, it is "a write-off". NME reports that other stores boycotted the release over the missing disc.

Why is there no disc?

It is a Take-Two decision. CEO Strauss Zelnick defended it to investors, as quoted by IGN via NME: "Our business is well over 90 per cent digitally distributed. It's already a digital business." For him, discs do not make sense for consumers on a big game, since you have to go online to play anyway. The confirmed facts: €79.99 for the standard edition and €99.99 for the digital-only Ultimate edition, detailed on our price page.

Will discs disappear?

NME notes that PlayStation has confirmed it will stop making game discs from early 2028, and that one report claims the move will hurt physical stores and the second-hand market. That comes from NME alone and we have not checked it elsewhere. For GTA 6, Rockstar does offer a physical item: the €399.99 collector's set The Goodtime State, which does not include the game, shown on our goodies page.

The contrast with launch figures is stark: $3.79 billion in retail sales announced by Rockstar for day one, much of it through console online stores.

Why it matters

For players, nothing changes: the game launches at home. For local retail, the message is harsh, as the biggest launch in entertainment history bypasses it. The figures here come from a handful of British shops and do not represent the whole market, but they raise the question of what shops become once discs disappear.
