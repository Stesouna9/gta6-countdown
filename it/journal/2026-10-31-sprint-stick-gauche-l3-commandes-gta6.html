GTA 6 sprint controls: left-stick click reported, full options still unclear

A report points to left-stick sprinting in GTA 6, but the complete controller layout and alternative settings remain undocumented.

Running in GTA 6 may ask players to learn a different thumb movement. La Crème Du Gaming, citing GameRant, reports that clicking the left stick would activate sprint instead of repeatedly pressing a face button. That is a report about the controls, rather than a complete controller guide. The source does not provide the full settings screen or a detailed Rockstar instruction sheet.

Which button would activate sprint?

According to La Crème Du Gaming, the input would be L3 on PS5 and the equivalent left-stick click on Xbox Series. We are keeping the attribution attached to that claim. Without the complete layout, it would be too early to turn a reported change into a definitive guide covering every movement option in the finished game.

There are several separate things to establish here. Starting a sprint is different from maintaining it, and both differ from the actions that cancel it. Naming a button does not settle all three. It also tells us nothing by itself about stamina, running speed or whether Jason and Lucia handle identically. Those details should not be inferred from a short description of an input.

Will the older input remain an option?

La Crème Du Gaming says the full control scheme has not been published and raises the possibility that a traditional input could remain available. That possibility is still unresolved. The consulted report does not supply a list of accessibility settings, button reassignment tools or alternative presets. Players who depend on a particular setup will need that information before judging the change.

There is a potential benefit to moving sprint away from repeated face-button presses: the right thumb could spend more time adjusting the camera. That is our reading of the proposed input, not a finding from a hands-on session. A stick click can require its own adjustment, and comfort will depend on the person holding the controller as well as the available options.

Why it matters

This is a question about an action players repeat constantly, rather than another activity to add to a feature list. Our earlier report on the pace of the simulation concerned realism; this one concerns how a player communicates with the game. The sensible next step is to look for documented settings, rather than buy accessories around an incomplete layout.
