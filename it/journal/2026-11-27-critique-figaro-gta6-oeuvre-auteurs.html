GTA 6: Le Figaro hails an auteur-driven game after 65 hours of play

Le Figaro calls GTA 6 “rich, funny, sensitive and touching” after 65 hours and writes that no AI was used to make it; the article is partly paywalled.

Fonti lefigaro.fr

Le Figaro has published its GTA 6 review, calling it a Rockstar “magnum opus”. The paper says it played 65 hours and argues the game is auteur-driven rather than a mere outlet for violence.

What does the review say?

The paper writes that GTA 6 is “rich, critical, funny, touching, moving, sensitive and intimate” and can make “the crudest vulgarity coexist with the gentlest poetry”. It says GTA 5 left it cold at the time and sees this sixth entry as closer to Red Dead Redemption, with a hyperrealism that blurs the line between virtual and real.

What does it say about production?

Le Figaro calls the game “a deeply human work”: it claims no artificial intelligence was used during a decade of production involving 4,500 people in studios worldwide. That is the paper's claim; we found no Rockstar confirmation in our sources. It also predicts this will probably be the last blockbuster made without AI.

The text is paywalled after the first paragraphs, so we quote only the accessible part. For context see our article on Lucia and the rest of the journal.

Why it matters

A major general-interest daily takes the game seriously as a work, against the violence controversy it cites itself. For the French public, it is one more sign GTA 6 is leaving the gaming niche.
