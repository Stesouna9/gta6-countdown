GTA 6 QR codes: scanning them leads to a fake FIB seizure page

Scanned with a real phone, a QR code shown in GTA 6 opens a parody FIB seizure notice on rockstargames.com, according to TweakTown.

Источники tweaktown.com

A QR code spotted in Vice City doesn't stop at the screen: scan it with a real phone and you land on a page of Rockstar's own site. According to TweakTown, Rockstar hid working QR codes in GTA 6, and the page they open is a parody seizure notice.

Where do GTA 6 QR codes lead?

The short link rsg.ms/qr1, which fan site Peekhole.de lists as a destination, redirects to a rockstargames.com page saying the site was "seized" by the Federal Investigation Bureau, the FIB, GTA's take on the FBI, with a warrant citing "47 U.S.C. § 11.25(A)" and a Vice City Police Department badge.

The page then jokes that, for lack of federal funding, the FIB is selling ad space on seized addresses. TweakTown says the charges and ads change per player or visit: one version cited organ trafficking and a "Dark Web Dating" ad, the one the writer loaded cited wire fraud and a pet delivery service offering black rhinos.

Who fell for it?

The outlet cites a clip of Spanish streamer Rubius shared by Movistar eSports, past 21.8 million views on X. In an in-game bathroom he scans a poster with his in-game phone, nothing happens, then with his real phone: a Spanish-language page appears. Streamer ohnePixel and others report the same codes on a "for sale" sign, a van and a motel poster.

Per Peekhole.de, the pages tie into Peekhole, the game's parody of creator subscription sites, whose QR stickers appear on cars around Leonida. For now one outlet describes the page, and Rockstar has not commented.

Should you scan these codes?

The destination is an official site, and TweakTown says the in-game codes are safe. Movistar eSports' rule still applies: never scan a code you don't recognize, especially on posters outside the game. Scam sites already feed on the game, as in our story on the fake "GTA VI Cloud Client".

Why it matters

Nothing changes in gameplay, but Rockstar extends its satire beyond the game: the joke moves from the fictional phone to the player's own. Pages that vary per visit hint at more surprises. Follow along in the journal.
