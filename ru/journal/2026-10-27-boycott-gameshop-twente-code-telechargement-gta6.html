GTA 6: Dutch shop boycotts the game because the box only holds a download code

Gameshop Twente in the Netherlands will not sell GTA 6 since the box contains only a download code, Gamekult reports.

Источники gamekult.com

A Dutch shop, Gameshop Twente, refuses to sell GTA 6 because the "physical edition" is only a box with a download code. According to Gamekult, which quotes store employee Floran Gelderman: "As long as it is only a code in a box, we are boycotting this release."

Why will this shop not sell GTA 6?

Rockstar has confirmed that no disc will be pressed: the box holds a code and nothing else. For Gameshop Twente, which focuses on discs, cartridges and retro gaming, that falls outside what it has defended for years. "We do not want to sell a download code to our customers as a physical product," Gelderman says, as quoted by Gamekult.

The choice has a cost, which Gamekult spells out: skipping GTA 6 means giving up revenue that most stores consider essential.

A wider worry about physical games

The seller describes a deeper trend: the shop now sells only a limited number of physical Xbox titles, and Nintendo sells more and more "codes in boxes". Gamekult writes that Sony would follow for good from 2028; we relay that as reported, with no confirmation of our own. For Gelderman, if owning a game depends on a publisher's servers, "you no longer fully own the game".

The same piece notes that Micromania in France is reinventing itself: merchandise with Zing, retro games, and a sale by Gamestop to a French-Quebec consortium. What happens to French store pre-orders is covered in our article on the looted Micromania stores, and prices at French retailers in our price comparison for France.

What buyers should keep in mind

The facts are simple. GTA 6 launches on November 19 on PS5 and Xbox Series X|S, at 79.99 euros for the standard edition and 99.99 euros for the Ultimate, digital only. The box sold in stores holds a code. All prices are on the price page.

Why it matters

One shop in the Netherlands is not a movement. Nothing suggests others will follow, and Gamekult names none. But the case shows the unease of specialist retailers over a game whose box has become packaging for a code. For players nothing changes: buying in store or online gets the same game, with the same pre-load on November 12.
