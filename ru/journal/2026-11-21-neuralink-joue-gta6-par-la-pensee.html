GTA 6: Neuralink shows a trial participant playing with thought alone

Neuralink posted a video on Friday of a clinical trial participant driving a speedboat in GTA 6 using only a brain implant, Numerama reports.

Фото: Rockstar Games

Источники numerama.com

One of the strangest images of launch week: on Friday 20 November, Neuralink posted a two-minute video on X showing a participant in its clinical trials playing GTA 6 without touching a controller, as Numerama reports. The game came out the day before on PS5 and Xbox Series X|S.

What does the video show?

The screen is split into several windows. In one, a motionless player steers a speedboat under a bridge, into a narrow tree-lined channel, then follows the objectives of a parcel-recovery mission. Beside it a virtual controller lights up with each command and a dashboard decodes brain activity live. The caption reads: "Playing GTA 6 with a Neuralink".

Neuralink gives neither the participant's identity nor the console, although the controller on screen looks like a PS5 DualSense, Numerama notes. The company is plainly using the launch of the year's most awaited game to showcase its implant.

How does it work?

The demo relies on Telepathy, the company's brain-computer interface. The implant picks up electrical signals from the part of the motor cortex that controls the hand. By imagining moving a hand or pressing a button, the patient produces a motor intention that algorithms turn into digital commands. The trials target people with quadriplegia after spinal cord injury, people with ALS, and brainstem stroke survivors. The first aim is restoring digital independence on a computer; some volunteers also test controlling consoles, keyboards or powered wheelchairs.

There is a precedent: British veteran Jon Noble was already playing World of Warcraft by thought in March, Numerama recalls. GTA 6 asks more, with driving, aiming and open-world missions, though the video says nothing about long-term precision or latency.

Worth remembering too: this is a video published by the company itself, with no independent test conditions. To our knowledge Rockstar has not reacted. For the rest of launch news, the journal is updated all day.

Why it matters

For the game, nothing. For accessibility, the demo shows video games serving as a testing ground for medical technology, and a title this popular speaking to everyone, including players the controller left out. What this technology brings outside clinical trials remains to be seen.
