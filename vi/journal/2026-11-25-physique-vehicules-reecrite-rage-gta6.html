GTA 6: Rockstar rewrote the physics of every vehicle, TechRadar interview says

Ben Lyons, Rockstar's technical director for physics, tells TechRadar the simulation of every vehicle category was fully redone for GTA 6.

Nguồn univers-simu.com

Rockstar has completely rewritten the vehicle physics simulation for GTA 6. The information comes from an interview with Ben Lyons, technical director for physics, published by TechRadar on 24 November 2026 and relayed by Univers-Simu.

What did Rockstar change about driving?

According to Univers-Simu, which cites the interview, the team redid the simulation for every vehicle category. Developers did not rely only on technical data or videos: some took part in preparing and running real racing cars. The site calls it one of the most interesting technical revelations since launch, especially for car simulation fans. Rockstar does not present GTA 6 as a simulator, and Univers-Simu says as much: it remains an action open world. The site also mentions technology close to that of specialist games for aircraft, without saying which, so I cannot say more. On driving itself, see our piece on the fastest car in the game.

What do we know about the RAGE engine?

GTA 6 runs on an evolution of Rockstar's in-house engine, RAGE (Rockstar Advanced Game Engine). Univers-Simu notes the studio has built its own technology for several console generations instead of using Unreal Engine or Unity. The name "RAGE 9" circulates in the press and community, but Rockstar has published no technical documentation confirming that numbering, so it is safer to speak of a new evolution of RAGE.

The first game to use RAGE was not a GTA but Rockstar Games Presents Table Tennis, released in 2006 on Xbox 360, again per Univers-Simu. The engine builds on technology from Angel Studios, which became Rockstar San Diego after Take-Two bought it. Rockstar wanted to move away from RenderWare, used for GTA III, Vice City and San Andreas.

Graphics engine or game engine?

The site draws the line: a graphics engine produces the image, while a game engine also handles memory, environment loading, physics, collisions, animation, AI, sound and lighting. RAGE belongs to the second group, which the site says explains why physics, weather, animation and character behavior constantly interact in Leonida.

Why it matters

For players nothing needs adjusting: it explains what you feel behind the wheel. The detail of the changes is unknown, since we do not have the original interview in front of us, only Univers-Simu's account. On the other technical story of the moment, see the PS5 and Xbox Series X comparison.
