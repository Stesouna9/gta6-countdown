GTA 6 harshest reviews: 30 fps cap, slow opening and upkeep chores

The lowest scores are 8s and 9s out of 10, yet PlayStation LifeStyle and ScreenHub flag real flaws: a 30 fps cap, a slow start and physical upkeep of the characters.

When a game's worst scores are 8s and 9s out of 10, the nuance lives in the details. Numerama went through the two harshest GTA 6 reviews, from PlayStation LifeStyle and ScreenHub, and pulled out three precise complaints. The game launches Thursday, November 19 on PS5 and Xbox Series X|S.

For context, the Metascore sits at 99, as covered in our reviews roundup. Here we look at what rubs.

Why is the 30 fps cap controversial?

PlayStation LifeStyle calls GTA 6 "the most technically ambitious title ever made" before docking a point for smoothness. According to Numerama, the game targets 30 frames per second on PS5, with dips to 20, and has no 60 fps Performance mode. The PS5 Pro is capped at 30 too. The reviewer says they avoided some dense districts because of slowdowns and saves a 10/10 for a possible PC or PS6 version. Rockstar has announced no PC version so far.

Is the opening too slow?

ScreenHub is harsher on pacing. The reviewer says they were "completely bored for the first twelve hours", describing chapter one as a "boyfriend simulator" in which Jason runs errands for Lucia. The Australian outlet says the game only opens up in chapter 3, around 25 hours in, and only feels playable rather than watchable in chapter 4, around 47 hours. The writing, it says, wavers between satire and social drama.

A third gripe: keeping Jason and Lucia physically fit through food and exercise, a mechanic inherited from Red Dead Redemption 2 and San Andreas.

Why it matters

Nothing changes for the date or price, listed on the release page. But these reviews help set expectations: a world both outlets praise, a 30 fps target unlikely to change before any patch, and an opening to take slowly. Both reviewers still praise Leonida, the lead duo and the level of detail.
