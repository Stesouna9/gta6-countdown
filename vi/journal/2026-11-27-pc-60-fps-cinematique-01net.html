GTA 6 PC 60 FPS claim concerns a cutscene, according to 01net

01net separates a 60 FPS cinematic from much slower reported gameplay and traces a separate 120 FPS clip to console footage.

Ảnh: Rockstar Games

Nguồn 01net.com

A 60 FPS counter is not evidence that an entire unofficial GTA 6 PC build runs at that speed. 01net reports that the figure was reached during an interrogation cutscene. Its account separates that demonstration from reported gameplay tests around 20 FPS, making the scene being measured central to understanding the claim.

What does the cutscene establish?

According to 01net, the headline number comes from a relatively quiet sequence. The outlet also describes fluctuations in busier cinematics and visible rendering problems. That is a narrower finding than sustained performance during free exploration, a busy drive or a complete mission. None of those wider outcomes follows automatically from the peak number.

Meaningful comparison begins with identifying the workload. A controlled camera shot and a player travelling through a city place different demands on a program. Even a clearly visible counter supplies only part of the evidence. Duration and circumstances matter if the question is whether the same performance persists through an ordinary session.

Why is the 120 FPS video a separate issue?

01net describes another clip as a console video signal sent to a computer using frame generation. The outlet says subsequent posts misleadingly presented that footage as a PC port demonstration. Displaying a game’s output on a computer and running the game itself on that computer are separate claims requiring separate evidence.

There is also a distinction between generating an intermediate picture and advancing the underlying game simulation. Without an account of the hardware, settings and video processing, the final display number cannot settle how the software actually performs. This is a general limitation of reading a benchmark from a short clip rather than a documented test.

Why it matters

The report clarifies a performance claim; it does not announce a commercial release. No official PC version is announced in the material available for this reporting round. Our release and platforms page provides context for separating official announcements from amateur projects. These demonstrations supply neither an official timetable nor a promise of stable performance.

The useful question is what a video establishes before its largest number is repeated. That approach leaves room to recognise reported technical progress while keeping the conclusion proportional to the evidence. A cutscene and a processed console signal can be interesting observations without becoming proof that general PC gameplay has reached the same frame rate.
