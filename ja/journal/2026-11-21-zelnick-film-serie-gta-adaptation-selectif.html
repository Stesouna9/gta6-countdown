GTA 6 on screen? Take-Two's boss does not rule it out but stays very selective

Take-Two president Strauss Zelnick does not exclude a GTA film or series, provided Rockstar's creative teams truly believe in it, Les Numériques reports.

写真：Rockstar Games

情報源 lesnumeriques.com

Could GTA ever be adapted for the screen? Asked on The David Rubenstein Show, Strauss Zelnick, president of Rockstar's parent company Take-Two, answered cautiously, as Les Numériques relates just after the release of GTA 6.

What did Strauss Zelnick say?

"We could make a film or a television series based on our intellectual property if our creative teams were truly committed to it. If they felt it would somehow spark interest and delight our consumers," he said (translated from the French report). He added: "The bar is set very high, and of course the risk of failure is always there. So we do not rule it out, but we are being very, very selective."

In short: no closed door, no announced project. The decision belongs to Rockstar's creatives, not shareholders, and on these remarks nothing is in the works.

Why is Rockstar reluctant?

This restraint is not new. Les Numériques recalls that Rockstar co-founder Dan Houser once explained an adaptation was unlikely: the studio did not want to lose creative control and worried about the series' reputation. Since then other games have succeeded on screen, with Super Mario Bros. in cinemas and The Last of Us or Fallout on TV. That changes the climate, not the substance: until the GTA team sees the point, there will be no film.

Context matters too. With $3.79 billion announced at launch, covered in our piece on sales and the release, Rockstar needs no film to exist. A bad one, though, could damage a brand that sells itself.

Why it matters

Nothing in the short term: no cast, studio or schedule. But Zelnick's "we do not rule it out" is slightly more open than the flat refusal of the past. Worth watching, without getting carried away.
