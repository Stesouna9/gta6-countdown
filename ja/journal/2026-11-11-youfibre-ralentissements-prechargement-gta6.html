GTA 6 preload: YouFibre reports congestion affecting Yorkshire customers

Yahoo Tech reports that YouFibre linked slower speeds in Yorkshire to GTA 6 downloads. The report describes a local network problem, rather than a worldwide outage.

写真：Rockstar Games

GTA 6 downloads are putting pressure on at least one British broadband provider, according to Yahoo Tech. Its report reproduces a YouFibre customer warning and a message attributed to chief executive Jeremy Chelot, describing congestion affecting Yorkshire customers. The useful detail is the scope: a reported capacity problem on one operator’s network, rather than evidence that the internet everywhere has stopped coping.

What has YouFibre told its customers?

As reported by Yahoo Tech, the provider told customers that high demand for GTA 6 downloads was affecting network capacity and reducing download speeds. Chelot’s message identifies Yorkshire as an affected area and says the company is working to rebalance traffic. Those details connect the slowdown to a particular network situation, with an operator attempting to address it.

The report does not establish how many customers are affected, how far their speeds have fallen or when normal service will return. It also does not measure conditions on other providers. Those gaps matter when assessing dramatic claims about the game overwhelming the internet: the available account supports a narrower conclusion about congestion for some subscribers.

Does record traffic also mean an outage?

Yahoo Tech separately reports that Virgin Media described its highest traffic peak and largest daily data volume during the preload period. That is a statement about demand. It should not automatically be treated as another outage, or merged with YouFibre’s warning into a single nationwide failure. Networks can report unusually heavy use without describing the same customer experience.

Nor does the British account determine download speeds for players in France. Our earlier look at broadband providers and downloading GTA 6 covers that separate question. We also covered the opening of console preloads. A connection’s advertised speed should not be read as a promise that every download will maintain it throughout.

Why it matters

Leaving time for a large download is more useful than assuming it will finish in one fixed window. For affected YouFibre customers, updates from the operator will be the relevant guide to recovery. For everyone else, this is a reason to allow some margin, rather than proof that their own connection will fail.
