GTA 6 Metacritic user score at 5.6 as fans hit Zelda, Gamekult says

GTA 6's Metacritic user score fell to 5.6/10 against a 99 critic Metascore, and Rockstar fans are retaliating against Zelda: Ocarina of Time, Gamekult reports.

情報源 gamekult.com

On Metacritic, GTA 6 shows a 99 Metascore from critics but a 5.6/10 user average, Gamekult reports. The gap comes from a wave of zero scores, which triggered retaliation from Rockstar fans against another game: the Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time remake.

Why is the GTA 6 user score so low?

According to Gamekult, Metacritic applied its usual 36-hour delay after launch before opening user reviews. Once it lifted, more than 10,000 reviews poured in and the average dropped to 5.6/10. The outlet notes that many of these 0/10s say nothing about technology or level design. They target the main duo's dynamic and Lucia's place in the story, some complaining the game plays like a "dating sim". Gamekult reads it as ideological review-bombing, in its own sharp tone.

Critics are united on their side: we covered the reviews in our piece on mission difficulty and the first player reactions.

What is happening to Zelda: Ocarina of Time?

Convinced Nintendo fans were behind the sabotage, Rockstar supporters hit back "zero for zero", Gamekult says. First target: the Ocarina of Time remake, which has a 95 critic Metascore and held the best user average on the aggregator until recently. Its pile of positive reviews keeps it at 8.3/10, but it has taken a wave of openly claimed 0/10s for several days. Some innocent games were caught in the crossfire, the outlet adds. We have no independent Metacritic figures to confirm these swings.

Do the zero scores say anything about the game?

Very little. An average pulled by coordinated zeros measures mobilisation, not quality. Critic scores number in the dozens and converge. Genuine player feedback is better read in discussions on our community page, where Lucia is debated too, but far from a barrage of 0/10s.

Why it matters

Nothing for the game, which tops the sales charts. For Metacritic, the case revives the question of moderating public scores after a hugely anticipated launch; the platform has announced no new measure so far, per our sources.
