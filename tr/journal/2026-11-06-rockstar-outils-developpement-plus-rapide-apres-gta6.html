GTA 6 tools could speed up future Rockstar games, says Aaron Garbut

Presse-citron reports that Rockstar expects its GTA 6 technology and team to support faster development, without naming a next game or release date.

Rockstar expects the technology and team assembled for GTA 6 to help it move faster on future projects, according to Presse-citron. The French outlet is reporting on Jason Schreier’s visit to the developer for Bloomberg, and attributes that assessment to Aaron Garbut. It is a statement about the studio’s foundations, with no release window attached to another Grand Theft Auto or Red Dead Redemption game.

That distinction matters when reading a headline about GTA 7 or Red Dead Redemption 3. Those names appear in Presse-citron’s framing, but the remarks it reproduces do not identify the next project. They cannot establish an announcement schedule, a production deadline or how long players will actually wait.

Does the gap after GTA 5 explain the next wait?

Presse-citron puts the discussion against the thirteen years between GTA 5 in 2013 and GTA 6. It also points out that Rockstar worked on GTA Online and Red Dead Redemption 2 during that period. The gap between numbered releases should therefore not be treated as thirteen years of uninterrupted work on a single title.

The outlet’s account of the reporting describes research in Miami, including time observing police, helicopter trips and visits to nightlife venues. These details explain some of the work behind Rockstar’s fictional city. They are not, by themselves, a list of playable activities or evidence that a future setting has already been selected.

What could carry over to another project?

In the remarks attributed to Garbut by Presse-citron, the engine, development tools and accumulated knowledge of the team form a foundation for moving forward. Having that base could leave more room to explore ideas instead of rebuilding every part of the process. The account offers no estimate for the time such an approach might save.

Presse-citron compares the idea with earlier periods when Rockstar released related games and expansions in succession. That is the outlet’s historical comparison, rather than an announced return to a particular release pattern. Our Buzz coverage had already flagged the broader discussion; the press report supplies context for assessing the claim.

Why it matters

The useful takeaway is an account of how Rockstar sees its development capacity after GTA 6. It does not tell us which game comes next, its scope, or whether its production will encounter different constraints. For players making immediate plans, the firm reference remains the GTA 6 release on November 19, 2026 for PS5 and Xbox Series X|S. No PC version has been announced. Future projects still need announcements of their own before expectations can become dates.
