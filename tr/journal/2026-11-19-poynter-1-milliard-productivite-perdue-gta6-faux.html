GTA 6: the $1 billion lost productivity claim can't be verified, says Poynter

Poynter found no survey or study behind the 1.5 million absent US workers and $1 billion loss claimed for GTA 6 launch day.

Kaynaklar poynter.org

For weeks one figure has circulated: GTA 6's release would cost the US economy $1 billion in productivity, with 1.5 million workers skipping work on November 19. The fact-checking team at Poynter looked for the source and found no published survey or study backing it.

Where does the GTA 6 billion-dollar figure come from?

The earliest reference Poynter found is a July 2025 article on the website of Spanish broadcaster Cadena SER. It cites José García-Montalvo, an economist at Pompeu Fabra University in Barcelona, but attributes neither the billion nor the 1.5 million to his interview, which mostly covered the profile of gamers likely to stay home: mainly men aged 18 to 30. The opening paragraph does mention $1 billion in lost productivity, without saying how it was calculated.

Poynter adds that the International Business Times published conflicting information: two articles say the figures should not be attributed to García-Montalvo, others credit him with the math. PolitiFact contacted him and had no reply at the time of publication.

What do economists say?

Nicholas Bloom, a Stanford economics professor, estimates that perhaps 5% of full-time US workers play Grand Theft Auto and only a fraction would take the day off rather than wait until after work. "I really don't believe these numbers unless it's backed up by a population-representative survey," he says, calling it social media clickbait. Useful context: the game had 17 million preorders as of November 18, per Poynter, a figure we followed in our Sensor Tower article.

Why it matters

The billion dollars is not a fact but an estimate with no public method. Mass absence on launch day is plausible; its priced cost is not. We will cite it as unverified from now on. For solid launch figures, see the release page.
