GTA 6 ads reach Times Square, Tokyo, Cologne and the Stade de France

Fan accounts on X report new GTA 6 advertising in Cologne, Times Square, Tokyo, Melbourne, Hong Kong and the Stade de France. Rockstar has not confirmed it.

Sumber x.com · x.com

With 19 days to go, GTA 6 advertising is spreading well beyond the United States. The account GTAVI_Countdown lists on X a giant banner in Cologne, a new full display in Times Square, several banners in Tokyo, a giant banner at the Stade de France, a mural being painted in Melbourne and a tram carrying Jason artwork in Hong Kong. These sightings come from fan accounts, not Rockstar, which has not detailed the campaign.

What do the pictures show?

The account GTA6Hub backs part of the list with two reader photos: a huge facade in Melbourne and a game-themed tram in Hong Kong. The post sums it up: the world is getting ready to join Vice City. The other spots (Cologne, Tokyo, Stade de France) rest for now on GTAVI_Countdown's list alone, with no photo cross-checked on our side.

The spread is the striking part: a Paris stadium, a New York crossroads, an Asian capital, a German city and Australia. No region is left out, which fits a simultaneous launch on November 19 on PS5 and Xbox Series X|S.

Is this new?

Not in principle. This week we covered the Miami-Dade campaign, which dressed a stadium and streets in Vice City colours, and the giant Bagnolet mural is already finished. What changes is the scale: from local, publicly funded decor to city-by-city advertising.

What is still unknown

Rockstar has not published a map of locations or a schedule. We do not know how long the displays stay up or whether more cities will follow. Pre-load is set for November 12 and release for November 19; the unlock time is still unknown, as our release page notes.

Why it matters

Nothing changes for the game itself, but visibility jumps: three weeks out, the brand is settling into public space on several continents. We will update this list if a photo or a Rockstar confirmation appears.
