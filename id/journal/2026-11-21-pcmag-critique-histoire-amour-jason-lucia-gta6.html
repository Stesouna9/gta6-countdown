GTA 6 review: PCMag praises Jason and Lucia’s love story, not the structure

After 100 hours, PCMag’s columnist calls GTA 6 “just another GTA” in structure, saved by the sincerity of Jason and Lucia’s relationship.

Sumber pcmag.com

One hundred hours in Vice City and beyond, and a mixed verdict. In an opinion piece published by PCMag, a writer says he feared a retread, then ranked the game among his favorites of the year for one reason: Jason and Lucia’s love story.

What does PCMag criticize in GTA 6?

The piece does not hide its reservations. According to PCMag, the game “isn’t some grand reimagining of GTA; it’s just another GTA”. The author describes déjà vu: missions that follow familiar beats, solid plans that always explode, the rags-to-riches arc from petty crime to corporate crime, and colorful quest givers with their own small storylines. He calls it “a borderline retro game, an accidental throwback”.

He also flags tone and representation. He praises several well-written Black characters, with the rap duo Real Dimez getting one of the best subplots, but regrets that some communities are framed as dangerous in a way the game treats as exciting.

Why does Jason and Lucia’s story change things?

Where the series’ cynicism lets you laugh at its characters without ever caring about them, PCMag argues GTA 6 is “sincere enough to actually make me care about what really matters”. The author warns of major spoilers and calls the romance the game’s secret weapon. The topic already stirred debate: see BFM’s take on the couple controversy and our piece on the nudity question.

A game awaited for over a decade

PCMag puts the flaws in context: more than a decade separates GTA 6 from GTA 5, and the only release since is GTA Online, which is not available at GTA 6’s launch. The writer says Rockstar had to play it safe after such a wait. The text we could read stops before its conclusion, so we add nothing to it.

Players are louder: the user score on Metacritic collapsed, as we covered in our review bombing article.

Why it matters
