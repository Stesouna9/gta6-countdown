GTA 6: a couple plays side by side and the prologue diverges with their choices

A Kotaku writer and his partner launched GTA 6 on two adjacent screens: their prologue splits within minutes, depending on how Lucia flees the police.

Sumber kotaku.com

On Kotaku, a writer describes playing GTA 6 at midnight with his partner, also a games journalist, each on their own TV, side by side. They did the same for Elden Ring and Tears of the Kingdom. With GTA 6, the appeal is seeing live what changes with each action.

What differs between two playthroughs?

The prologue is the example. Lucia must get back to a coffee shop while police comb the block. The author's partner, more pragmatic, walks the sidewalks until officers pick her up. He cuts around and swims, gets hit on the head by a police boat and is knocked out.

The next cutscene differs: for him, Lucia is handcuffed to a gurney by EMS and police and pushed into an ambulance; for her, she is manhandled into a police car. In both she pleads to return to the coffee shop, but his version felt more fraught, he writes. Later, watching the other enter an impound lot as Jason in a different way, they traded tips.

What should we take from this?

It is a personal account, not a methodical test: one precise example, two playthroughs. It does confirm that consequences show up very early, as other players noted with NPCs reacting after a cut-off.

Nothing from Rockstar: no screen-sharing or co-op feature is involved, only two consoles and two copies. But it hints at how replayable the opening hours are. For related tips, see how game sharing works on PS5 and Xbox.
