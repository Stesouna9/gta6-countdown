GTA 6 on PS5: false-region accounts raise questions over PSN rules

20 Minutes highlights the risks of false PSN registration details, without establishing a new Sony enforcement policy specific to GTA 6.

A PlayStation account registered with a false New Zealand address could expose a GTA 6 player to account penalties, according to 20 Minutes. The French newspaper's November 8 report points to general PlayStation Network rules. It does not supply a new Sony announcement specifically targeting the game's launch or evidence of a wave of related bans.

What is the issue with a false account region?

The concern described by the newspaper is the accuracy of the information supplied when registering an account. In the scenario it discusses, a player provides an address in a country where they do not actually live. That raises a question about compliance with the service's terms, rather than simply changing the time shown on a console.

20 Minutes mentions temporary and permanent restrictions among the possible responses to rule violations. Its report does not establish which particular penalty would follow in an individual GTA 6 case. It provides no tally of affected accounts and no documented enforcement decision involving this launch. Those missing details matter when interpreting the warning.

Does the Xbox discussion apply to PS5?

Readers of our earlier coverage of the New Zealand Xbox discussion should keep the platforms separate. An approach described for one system does not establish an equivalent route on another. The newspaper's PlayStation example involves account registration, regional purchasing conditions and the way access to a game is distributed.

The report also mentions VPN use as something players might consider. It does not demonstrate that a VPN alone unlocks GTA 6 early for a French PlayStation account. Neither a location change nor the existence of an earlier time zone should therefore be treated as a verified promise of access. Our release information page collects the broader launch details.

Why it matters

The useful distinction is between a release schedule and permission to use a service in a particular way. A game becoming available earlier somewhere else does not, on its own, answer questions about the information a player gives a platform. The newspaper's warning addresses that second issue.

This is consequently a reason to read the regional account discussion carefully, rather than evidence of a special GTA 6 enforcement operation. The material published by 20 Minutes establishes neither an automatic punishment for every attempt nor a reliable early-access procedure for PS5. Its account of the general rules should not be expanded into either claim.
