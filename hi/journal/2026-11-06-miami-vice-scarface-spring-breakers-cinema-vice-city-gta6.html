GTA 6 Vice City influences: from Miami Vice and Scarface to Spring Breakers

Gamekult traces the films and shows that shaped Vice City, from Miami Vice and Scarface to Spring Breakers, seen as a likely benchmark for GTA 6.

Vice City was never Miami: it was a show about Miami, itself inspired by a film about Miami shot mostly in California. That is the argument of a long piece by French outlet Gamekult, published weeks before GTA 6 launches, tracing the film lineage of Rockstar's city.

Why does Vice City not look like the real Miami?

According to Gamekult, it started in 2002 with Vice City: Rockstar cast Philip Michael Thomas, who played Ricardo Tubbs in Miami Vice, as Lance Vance. The outlet reads it as a sign that the game never aimed to recreate the real city but to rebuild a TV series, dreamed up in Rockstar North's Edinburgh offices.

Gamekult recalls Michael Mann's rule on the NBC series launched in 1984: no earth tones, pastel everywhere. Scarface (1983), directed by Brian De Palma and written by Oliver Stone, supplied the other half of the myth with Tony Montana's rise. The site notes an irony: the film left Miami for California in 1982 after a city official, relaying Cuban exiles' anger, threatened filming permits.

Which references already appear in the 2002 game?

Per Gamekult, the Vercetti estate mimics Tony Montana's mansion down to the final shootout, the Malibu Club copies the Babylon, and a hidden apartment holds a blood-stained bathroom with a chainsaw identical to the one in Scarface. The opening theme nods to the Miami Vice credits, and the cast includes Ray Liotta, Dennis Hopper and Burt Reynolds.

Which cinema weighs on GTA 6 today?

The piece argues film has since put that fantasy on trial: Mann's 2006 version swapped pastel for digital video, and Florida became American cinema's asylum with No Pain No Gain or The Florida Project. Above all, Gamekult sees Spring Breakers (2012, Harmony Korine), which takes the Miami Vice palette and doses it with acid, as the likely benchmark for GTA 6. That is Gamekult's reading, not a Rockstar statement. See also our music page and the recent article on Vice City ads in Miami.

Why it matters

Nothing changes in the game itself: this is analysis, not an announcement. But it offers a lens ahead of the November 19 release on PS5 and Xbox Series X|S for spotting what comes from the series, from Scarface or from Spring Breakers' saturated neon.
