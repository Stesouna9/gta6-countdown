GTA 6: five TV settings to change on PS5 and Xbox Series

HDMI 2.1 port, Game mode, VRR: per Frandroid, five settings take minutes and get the most out of GTA 6 on PS5 and Xbox Series.

Fontes frandroid.com

A new TV doesn't guarantee a good picture. By default it applies processing that delays the image and shifts colours, says Frandroid, which lists five settings to change in order, in the TV and console menus.

Which HDMI port and cable?

First: plug the console into an HDMI 2.1 port, often labelled "4K 120 Hz", "144 Hz", "165 Hz" or "Game". Frandroid says the "eARC" label isn't enough: it covers audio return, and on some sets that port can't carry 4K at 120 Hz. You also need an Ultra High Speed HDMI cable, as an older one can block 4K at 120 Hz.

What does Game mode do?

Second: Game mode, called Game Optimizer on LG. It turns off part of the image processing and cuts latency, the delay between pressing a button and the on-screen reaction. If the TV supports ALLM it switches on its own, but Frandroid advises checking the menu.

Does VRR help at 30 fps?

Barely. Rockstar targets 30 fps on all consoles, PS5 Pro included, with no performance mode at launch. VRR syncs the panel to the game's real rate, but its range usually starts at 40 or 48 Hz. Leaving it on Automatic is harmless, Frandroid notes. Rockstar's Marc Chambers ruled out any 40 or 60 fps mode on Wednesday, citing the CPU; details in our 30 fps article.

Why it matters

None of these settings adds frames, but they cut input lag and avoid skewed colours, which counts in a game with so much driving. Allow a few minutes before you start. For the rest of today's news, see the journal.
