GTA 6: Jason and Lucia's romantic turn divides some fans, Le Figaro reports

Buying flowers and dropping sex workers: Le Figaro says GTA 6's romantic turn angers some fans, while Rockstar defends the story of a couple on the run.

Sources lefigaro.fr · thewomensvoices.fr

Buying flowers for a partner, fixing up the house, no more sex workers: GTA 6's romantic turn has angered some of the series' fans. Le Figaro reports that some players accuse Rockstar of adding "feminist mush", while The Women's Voices sees a row driven by influencers from the American manosphere.

What do these players object to?

According to Le Figaro, French rapper Leto dropped the controller during a Twitch stream with streamer Anyme, refusing to play "la racli", slang for a girl. On X, one player called the game "CUCK simulator", a term from masculinist circles. The Women's Voices adds that US streamer Asmongold used the same phrase, and that Myron Gaines of the Fresh & Fit podcast argued on 21 November that gaming should stay reserved for men. The site says Media Matters for America logged those remarks on 24 November.

What changed in the game?

Le Figaro describes a new honor system that rewards good behavior and discourages pointless violence. Players must keep Jason and Lucia's relationship alive, keep both in shape to pull off heists, and earn focus points through acts of civic decency. Per the specialist press quoted by the paper, looking after the couple and staying a "professional" criminal rather than a "violent" or "psychopath" one is needed to unlock the best ending. For the first time since GTA III in 2001, sex workers are absent. We previously covered the game's possible endings.

Violence is still everywhere: The Women's Voices points out that GTA 6 keeps explosions, crime, nudity and an adult world. The disappearance of transgression is not the issue, the site writes; some depictions of couples have simply moved.

What does Rockstar say?

In a statement quoted by Le Figaro, the studio says "GTA 6 is a game that tells the story of a couple in a difficult situation, lovers on the run" and that everything added was meant "to serve the spirit of the story we wanted to tell". Andrew Przybylski of the Oxford Internet Institute calls it a story "people can engage with in a deeper, and in some ways more thoughtful way", without seeing a less transgressive game. Le Figaro also notes the game itself mocks masculinist influencers.

Why it matters

Nothing on sales: Rockstar claims $3.8 billion on day one, as covered in our journal. But the row shows GTA 6 is now discussed as a social phenomenon, not just a game. The real scale of the rejection is unknown: both pieces cite a few very visible voices, with no figure for the share of players involved.
