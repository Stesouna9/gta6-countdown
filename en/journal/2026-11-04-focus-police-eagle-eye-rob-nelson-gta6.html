GTA 6 Focus mode highlights police, Rob Nelson explains in IGN interview

Rob Nelson demonstrates Focus by highlighting a nearby officer in IGN’s interview, comparing the tool with Red Dead Redemption 2’s Eagle Eye.

Sources ign.com · gamerant.com · opencritic.com

GTA 6 has a tool for noticing danger before walking straight into it. In IGN's interview, Rockstar North co-studio head Rob Nelson demonstrates Focus by making a nearby police officer stand out. GameRant revisits the demonstration today. We checked the original interview: Nelson himself makes the comparison with Eagle Eye from Red Dead Redemption 2, rather than leaving that connection to fan interpretation.

What does Focus actually do?

The observed example is straightforward: an officer becomes easier to distinguish in the scene. Nelson describes the tool as helping players locate things they are looking for on the map, including police. That establishes an observation function, but leaves its full range unclear. This part of the interview does not provide a complete list of targets, restrictions or configuration options.

It would therefore be premature to describe Focus as an ability that reveals everything. The useful point is narrower: the player has another way to read a situation before making a move. In a crowded street, knowing where an officer is could influence a route or a pause. How useful that information becomes will depend on the rules surrounding the ability and the circumstances in which it can be activated.

How does it connect to escaping police?

In the surrounding exchange, IGN records Nelson explaining that officers can know the vehicle being driven and that they are seeking a couple. Switching vehicles or splitting up can help reduce that awareness. An observation tool fits naturally into those decisions. Identifying an officer might help someone choose when to move, although the demonstration does not promise that Focus automatically provides a successful escape.

The Eagle Eye comparison identifies a familiar point of reference for Red Dead Redemption 2 players. It does not establish that every rule carries across unchanged. Combat footage involving slowed time raises a separate question: whether the observation feature and that shooting effect belong to the same system. GameRant explicitly leaves this connection unresolved, and the quoted interview passage does not settle it.

Why it matters

This is a small but concrete addition to our understanding of GTA 6: police encounters may involve reading a scene as well as reacting quickly. What remains unknown includes the ability's range, any resource cost and its activation conditions. Those questions need fuller explanation or hands-on evidence.
