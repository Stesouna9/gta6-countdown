GTA 6 preload: Orange reports 19 Tbps peak below its network record

Orange told Numerama that total network traffic peaked at 19 Tbps during preloading, below its roughly 23 Tbps record. That is not a nationwide measurement.

Sources numerama.com

A huge game download does not automatically set a network record. Orange told Numerama that traffic during GTA 6 preloading reached about 19 Tbps, below its roughly 23 Tbps maximum. The report offers a useful French data point after concerns about the demands the release could place on internet infrastructure.

Does 19 Tbps represent GTA 6 alone?

No. Numerama describes an aggregate measurement covering all activity on Orange’s network at the time. It cannot be treated as a dedicated counter for Rockstar downloads. It also says nothing directly about the speed available to an individual console, which is a different measurement with a different practical meaning.

The geographical conclusion needs equal care. Figures from France’s other major providers were not available in the report. Orange staying below its maximum does not establish that every French connection worked smoothly. Equally, an isolated slow download would not demonstrate that the whole national network had reached its limit.

Why spread the demand over time?

Orange credits the staggered download window and overnight opening for helping manage demand, according to Numerama. The underlying idea is straightforward: a large transfer has less competition when it happens outside an already busy period. That explanation concerns how demand is distributed, rather than a promise about performance for every household.

Our earlier coverage of YouFibre’s reported slowdowns addressed another provider. Different experiences can exist at the same time. The challenge for slower broadband users also remains distinct from the question of aggregate capacity. A download can take a long time without setting any nationwide record.

Why it matters

Preparing for the GTA 6 release involves several separate bottlenecks: storage on the console, the household connection and the provider’s infrastructure. The Orange measurement mainly informs the last of those. It should not be used to calculate an individual completion time or predict how online services will behave when players arrive.

The sensible takeaway is limited but useful. Significant demand can fit below an existing peak, and scheduling can help explain why. A broader French assessment would need comparable evidence from more providers. Until then, Orange’s reported result is one documented observation, rather than a verdict on every network or every player’s experience.
