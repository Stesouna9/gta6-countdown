GTA 6: Cyberleek posts new Lucia videos and demands a disc release

Cyberleek has posted new clips centred on Lucia and threatens to release the full story if its token hits $30 million, Mashable reports.

Sources mashable.com · eteknix.com

Cyberleek is back. According to Mashable, the anonymous GTA 6 leaker posted new clips over the weekend and on Monday morning, centred on Lucia, following last week's 24-minute video. We are not linking to them and will not describe what they show.

What does Cyberleek want from Take-Two?

The leaker put a warning on their website: if the group's token reaches a $30 million market cap, a video of the full story playthrough will go out before launch. They also demand that Take-Two apologise for not releasing GTA 6 on disc and fix that decision. eTeknix, which spotted the post on Reddit, notes there is no proof of a playable build, that neither Rockstar nor Take-Two has replied, and that the post does not say which asset the target applies to, so nobody can tell how close it is.

Mashable calls the demand unrealistic: starting disc production a month before launch is not feasible, and nothing suggests the publisher is negotiating. Our October 14 story reported an "End of the road" message announcing the end of the leaks. The new clips contradict that promise, or at least the "for now" attached to it.

Are these videos representative of the final game?

Probably not, says Mashable. As in the Jason clips, NPCs do not react to Lucia's nudity. Recent Rockstar games are built around believable NPC behaviour, so the writer concludes the scene is not a normally accessible feature and was produced with developer tools. That is a reading, not a Rockstar confirmation.

The caution from eTeknix still applies: supposed leaked builds are a common lure for malware, and a development build would run badly on an ordinary PC. No PC version has been announced anyway.

Why it matters

Almost nothing for the game itself, a lot for the mood: spoilers keep creeping toward November 19, which you can follow on the release page. Our advice stays the same: mute the usual keywords and download nothing. We also covered the token's collapse; its fate will show whether the $30 million threat still means anything.
