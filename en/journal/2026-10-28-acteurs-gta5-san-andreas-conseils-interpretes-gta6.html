GTA 6: GTA V and San Andreas actors share advice for the next leads

The veterans discuss Rockstar’s guidance and the lasting connection between performers and fans.

Sources gamesradar.com

J-20 GTA 6 releases in 20 days →

The performers behind GTA V and San Andreas have offered advice to the next GTA leads, with one recurring theme: be ready for a long relationship with the audience. GamesRadar+ reports that Ned Luke, Shawn Fonteno, Steven Ogg and Young Maylay discussed the subject with All Out Gaming at MCM Comic Con. Their comments concern life after release, when a role can follow its performer far beyond the recording sessions.

Advice from the previous generation

According to GamesRadar+, Michael actor Ned Luke encourages the newcomers to enjoy themselves. Young Maylay, who played CJ in San Andreas, emphasizes preparation. Franklin actor Shawn Fonteno recommends following Rockstar's guidance and trusting the studio to steer them through what comes next. Taken together, their advice sounds more practical than glamorous: enjoy the job, listen and prepare for its lasting effects.

None of those remarks reveals a mission, a running time or an official casting announcement for GTA VI. The subject is the work surrounding a performance once the game is in players' hands. A familiar character can turn an actor into someone fans want to meet years later, and the veterans at the convention have personal experience of that transition.

The audience grows older too

As GamesRadar+ recounts, Maylay describes meeting now gray-haired visitors who still recognize him as CJ. That observation gives the discussion its weight. Players do not necessarily move on from a protagonist when the next installment appears. They carry the memory into later life, sometimes returning to conventions to meet the person associated with it.

Maylay also reassures the GTA V cast that the next generation will not simply replace them, the report explains. His own connection with fans survived the arrival of Michael, Franklin and Trevor. That is a useful perspective on a series whose next release attracts so much attention: new characters can join the shared history without removing the people who helped build it.

Why it matters

With November 19 approaching, the exchange draws attention to the performers behind the protagonists. It is not confirmation of who plays Jason or Lucia. GamesRadar+ describes those roles as still officially unannounced in this context, so fan identifications should remain separate from a studio casting statement.

Our GTA VI journal follows that distinction when covering interviews and official announcements. The news here is a public handover of experience, rather than a fresh clue about the story. The older cast members know that a GTA performance can remain part of an actor's life for decades. Their advice gives the newcomers a grounded picture of what that recognition may involve once players meet the characters.
