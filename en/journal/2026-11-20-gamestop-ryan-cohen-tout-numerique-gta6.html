GTA 6: GameStop CEO slams the all-digital release and the dying used market

Windows Central reports that GameStop CEO Ryan Cohen says GTA 6's all-digital launch has hurt players and shops, which lose the resale market.

Sources windowscentral.com

GameStop's boss did not wait for launch day to say what he thinks of Rockstar's choice. According to Windows Central, Ryan Cohen said on the eve of release that the move to all-digital "has hurt consumers" and his own chain, just as GTA 6 breaks sales records without a disc.

The picture has two sides. On one, the biggest video game retailer in the US. On the other, small UK shops, including the Leicester store already covered from the BBC report: five copies sold in total.

What is GameStop's complaint about an all-digital GTA 6?

Speaking to Bloomberg, as relayed by Windows Central, Cohen argues that swapping discs for $80 downloads and boxes holding only a code takes basic rights away from players. His line: "When you buy a game, you should be able to sell it, trade it or lend it to a friend."

He adds that publishers want the used market dead because "removing a used copy eliminates an alternative to buying new". For GameStop that matters: per the site, pre-owned and refurbished goods made up 22% of its sales in the quarter ended August 1, about $171 million, down 32% year on year.

Did shops sell boxes?

Hardly. The owner of Super Game Shack in Leicester calls his numbers "embarrassing" and says that for game shops the release is "a write-off", even though he rates the game as "amazing". A collector interviewed by the BBC also mourns the end of launch parties and Saturday-morning store runs. Windows Central notes the old midnight queues did not happen in US stores either.

Context matters here: Sony announced in July that it will end PlayStation disc production by January 2028, the outlet says. For French buyers, prices stay those of the digital edition, 79.99 euros standard and 99.99 euros Ultimate, and the disc question is a collector's curiosity rather than a way to play.

Why it matters

Rockstar has not answered the criticism in the sources we read. What is established: GTA 6 is the biggest launch the industry has seen, and it sells with no playable physical media. What is debated: the place of resale, and of shops, when the biggest games are files. The argument goes beyond GTA 6, but this launch gives it its first hard numbers.
