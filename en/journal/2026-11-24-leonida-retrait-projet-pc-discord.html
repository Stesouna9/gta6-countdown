GTA 6 PC project Leonida withdrawn amid reported Discord removals

Notebookcheck reports Leonida’s withdrawal, while alleged Rockstar intervention remains publicly unconfirmed.

Sources notebookcheck.net · tweaktown.com

The unofficial effort to bring GTA 6 to PC has suffered a reported setback. Notebookcheck says Leonida’s developer is stepping away and that the project’s GitHub page and Discord server are no longer accessible. The outlet links the development to possible action by Rockstar, while noting that the company has not publicly commented on the reported removals.

What happened to Leonida?

According to Notebookcheck, developer ItsNotRexed cited circumstances outside his control. Leonida was an open-source static recompilation project intended to run the Xbox version natively on PC. Its withdrawal is a separate development from the release of the missing Xbox executable, which our earlier coverage already addressed.

The developer’s wording does not establish who requested the removal or explain the precise circumstances. The report also cites user accounts of other GTA 6 Discord communities disappearing. Those claims need to remain attributed: a server becoming unavailable is not, by itself, public proof of a coordinated enforcement campaign by Rockstar. No definitive explanation from the publisher is included in the material reviewed here.

Does reaching a settings screen mean the port is playable?

No. In its account of the early technical progress, TweakTown describes initial settings, legal notices and warning screens, with no playable areas shown. Reaching those stages is different from loading the world, controlling a character and running the game reliably over time.

Our earlier overview of Leonida and Xbox2Windows concerned experimental work. The newly reported startup progress changes that technical picture, but it does not establish a finished PC release. Nor does one project’s disappearance tell us whether every other attempt has stopped.

Why it matters

There are two stories here: reported technical progress and the withdrawal of a named project. Neither supplies an official PC launch date. Players waiting for that platform still need a publisher announcement rather than an executable file, a screenshot or a promised timetable from an unofficial community. Our release information page follows practical availability separately from these experiments. The public explanation remains incomplete at this stage. Any stronger claim about Rockstar’s role in the removals would require additional evidence.
