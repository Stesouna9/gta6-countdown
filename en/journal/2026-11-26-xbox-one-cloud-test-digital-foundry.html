GTA 6 on Xbox One: Digital Foundry finds cloud frame pacing issues

Digital Foundry reports limited added latency in its cloud test, alongside uneven frame delivery compared with a local Series S.

Sources digitalfoundry.net

Digital Foundry has tested GTA 6 on Xbox One through Xbox Cloud Gaming and found a workable experience with a significant presentation flaw. Its report praises image quality and limited additional latency under the test conditions, while highlighting uneven frame delivery. The distinction is fundamental: the game runs remotely on Microsoft's servers, rather than as a native Xbox One release.

Which game version does the cloud provide?

Digital Foundry identifies the streamed game as the Xbox Series S version. It describes a 720p base image reconstructed to 1080p with FSR 4.1, retaining major effects including ray-traced lighting and reflections. These are characteristics of the remotely executed version, not evidence that the Xbox One's own graphics hardware is rendering those effects locally.

The outlet reports frame rates generally ranging through the twenties to around thirty, depending on CPU load. Its larger concern is frame pacing: images arrive less evenly than on a local Series S. Similar average frame rates can therefore feel different in motion. Digital Foundry says it has reported the issue to Microsoft, which is looking into it.

Why the network conditions matter

The test found roughly nine milliseconds of extra latency compared with local Series S play. That measurement came from an Ethernet-connected console on a fast fibre connection, using a wired controller. Digital Foundry cautions that location, connection quality and other parts of the input and display chain can alter the experience for different players.

The result is evidence that a small additional latency cost is possible in those conditions. It is not a promise for every home network. The report also describes minor compression artefacts in fine detail such as foliage and chain-link fencing, noting that these are more difficult to notice when the scene is moving.

Why it matters

For Xbox One owners, the analysis documents a way to access GTA 6 without running it locally on a current-generation console. It does not make Xbox One a native GTA 6 platform. Our release and platforms page provides context for separating the game's released console versions from ways of reaching them through remote services.

Digital Foundry still recommends local play for people who already own a Series S or Series X. For everyone considering the older console as a streaming device, the sensible comparison is the experience on their actual connection, together with the service's access conditions. Frame delivery, compression and response all matter alongside the headline frame rate, and the reported pacing problem remains a meaningful qualification to the otherwise positive test.
