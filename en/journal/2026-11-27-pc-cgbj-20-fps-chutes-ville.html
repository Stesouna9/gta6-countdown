GTA 6 PC footage reportedly reaches 20 FPS, then falls near 10 in town

Pause Hardware describes CGBJ footage with coastal gameplay around 20 FPS and much slower city scenes. The test hardware has not been disclosed.

Sources pausehardware.com

New footage offers a more specific look at the progress and shortcomings of unofficial GTA 6 projects on Windows. Pause Hardware describes a two-minute video attributed to a CGBJ build, with coastal gameplay near twenty frames per second before a substantial drop in town. The missing hardware information is central to interpreting that result: viewers cannot tell what equipment produced it or how another PC would compare.

What happens beyond the menu?

According to Pause Hardware, the counter shows thirty frames per second in menus and roughly nineteen to twenty-three while travelling along the coast by jet ski or car. In the city, it falls to around ten. Those are observations from different scenes, not a sustained performance guarantee. Quoting only the strongest number would erase precisely the variation that makes the footage useful.

The report also describes missing water in parts of the coastline and magenta trails covering a road. Rendering errors matter alongside speed: a higher counter does not tell us whether the scene is correct, whether every mission works or whether a session will remain stable. Nothing in this short demonstration establishes that the broader game can be completed reliably on Windows.

Can other projects provide a direct comparison?

Pause Hardware identifies VIctory as the underlying recompilation effort behind CGBJ, using leaked Xbox code. It also cites another project working with a Series S profile and an internal resolution of 960 × 540 before upscaling. Those settings change the workload substantially. Without matching scenes, hardware and rendering conditions, their frame counters cannot sensibly become a single ranking of PC performance.

Read also GTA 6 PC launcher and debug menu shown in unofficial demonstrations

The report says the repository associated with CGBJ became inaccessible, while explicitly leaving the reason unknown. An unavailable page is not enough to establish another Take-Two removal. Earlier coverage documented the first reported 3D rendering and subsequent mission progress at low frame rates. This development adds a more detailed scene-by-scene account rather than changing the status of an official PC edition.

Why it matters

The footage, as reported, helps explain both the pace of community experimentation and the distance still separating it from a dependable release. It provides no sound basis for a shopping list or a promise of twenty frames per second on a particular machine. Rockstar has announced no PC timetable through these projects.
