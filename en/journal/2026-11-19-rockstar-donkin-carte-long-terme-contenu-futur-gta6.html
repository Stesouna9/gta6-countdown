GTA 6 map: Rockstar saved unused areas for future content, says Donkin

Rockstar's Stephen Donkin says the GTA 6 map was "built for the long term", with areas kept for future gameplay or narrative needs.

Sources mashable.com

GTA 6's map holds unused areas, and Rockstar says so. According to Mashable, citing a Kotaku interview, Rockstar VP of design Stephen Donkin said: "The map has been built for the long term and with many factors in mind. So there are a few areas on the map that give us options in the future should we have a gameplay or narrative need for them."

What did Kotaku's review reveal?

Reviewer Zack Zwiezen played more than 130 hours without visiting some places, Mashable reports: a zoo and two large casinos where gambling is not allowed, never mentioned in the story or side missions. Donkin responded to that.

Single-player content or an online mode?

Mashable reads the quote as a sign of future additions, without saying which: new single-player missions or a still-unannounced online mode. That is the outlet's reading, not a Rockstar promise; nothing is confirmed. GTA 5 never got single-player DLC despite 2013 promises, as GTA Online took priority.

An already huge map

Rockstar told Game Informer in September it is its largest map, roughly twice GTA 5's and three times Red Dead Redemption 2's, with six regions: Vice City, Leonida Keys, Grassrivers, Port Gellhorn, Ambrosia and Mount Kalaga National Park. See the Leonida map leak and the Game Informer interview.
