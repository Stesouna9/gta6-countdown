GTA 6 hacker Arion Kurtaj jailed for seven years over 2022 leak

Arion Kurtaj, the Lapsus$ member who stole 90 GTA 6 videos from Rockstar in 2022, was sentenced to seven years in London, the Oxford Mail reports, but walked free.

Sources oxfordmail.co.uk · independent.co.uk · bbc.com · x.com

Arion Kurtaj, the hacker who stole GTA 6 footage from Rockstar in 2022, was sentenced to seven years in prison on Monday, the Oxford Mail reports. The 21-year-old from Oxford, a member of the Lapsus$ group, appeared at Woolwich Crown Court in London. He had already spent time in custody and, according to the paper, walked free from court.

The Independent and ITVX carry the story, and the BBC says he was released on licence after serving most of the sentence. Mr Justice Turner told him: "You are obviously a very talented young man. Don't waste those talents by spending the rest of your life in and out of prison."

What was Arion Kurtaj accused of in the Rockstar case?

Per the Oxford Mail, he hacked Rockstar Games and took 90 gameplay videos of GTA VI, released online as TeaPotUberHacker, while threatening to publish the source code. The BBC adds that he warned on the studio's internal Slack that he would start releasing the code if Rockstar did not contact him on Telegram within 24 hours. Rockstar refused. The studio says recovery cost it $5m plus thousands of staff hours.

He pleaded guilty last week to fraud and Computer Misuse Act offences. The attacks ran from August 2021 to September 2022 and also hit BT, EE, Revolut and Uber, where the Oxford Mail says costs exceeded $2.8m.

Why is he free despite a seven-year sentence?

Given a hospital order in 2023 because of his autism, he was found fit to plead in 2025 and moved to prison, the BBC writes. Proceedings then resumed. The sentence accounts for time already served. The BBC also says he is under a five-year Serious Crime Prevention Order barring him from Telegram and Discord.

What it means for GTA 6

Nothing for the game, released on 19 November: this closes the legal chapter of the 2022 leak. Other threats have circulated since, such as Cyberleek's claims about an executable; no link to this case has been established. More in the journal.
