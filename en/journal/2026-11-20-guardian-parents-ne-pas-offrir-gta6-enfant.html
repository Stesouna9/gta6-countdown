GTA 6: The Guardian urges parents not to buy it for their children

Guardian writer Keza MacDonald calls on parents not to buy GTA 6 for kids: it is rated 18 and, in her view, the most explicit game she has played.

Sources theguardian.com

GTA 6 has been out since midnight in France, and The Guardian has run a blunt warning. Under the headline "Parents: please don't buy Grand Theft Auto VI for your child", Keza MacDonald calls the game brilliant but says it is the most explicit she has ever played.

She starts from an anecdote: at her stepson's ninth birthday party in 2014, a child asked her to put on GTA V. His mother did not know an 18 rating flags adult content, not difficulty.

What does the Guardian object to in GTA 6?

MacDonald describes an exhaustively detailed virtual Florida full of crime. She lists the violence (executions, brutal police raids, torture), but stresses the sex above all: in her words, far more than any earlier GTA, with full-frontal nudity, scenes between the two leads and a browsable in-game adult site on a character's laptop.

She adds drugs, everywhere at Vice City parties, and crude humour even on the in-game radio and TV. Her verdict: a 10- or 12-year-old cannot put this in context, and if you think a child is too young for Breaking Bad, they are too young for GTA 6. She frames it as her opinion and that of ratings boards.

What does the PEGI 18 rating mean for parents?

Rockstar's PEGI 18 rating bars sales to minors in principle. The Guardian's point lands harder because the game is digital only: even the boxed copy holds just a download code, so checks at the till are less obvious. Console parental controls are the simplest fix. We covered the school side with British schools worried about absences.

What the piece does not give: no data on how many players are minors, and no new Rockstar measure beyond the rating. It is an opinion column, not an investigation.

Why it matters

Nothing changes in the game, since the rating has been known for months. But a major British daily running this the day after launch revives the parental responsibility debate as sales soar. See our release page for the rest of the launch facts.
