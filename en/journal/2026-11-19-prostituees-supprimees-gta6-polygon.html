GTA 6 drops hirable sex workers for the first time since GTA 3, Polygon says

Polygon reports that the hirable prostitutes seen in the series since 2001 do not return in GTA 6, a game built around a couple's story.

Sources polygon.com

This is a deep change for the series. According to Polygon, the sex workers players could pay in Grand Theft Auto games since 2001 do not return in GTA 6. The outlet calls the game "unusually realistic" and says it tells "a humane story" about a couple, and that this approach shows in the actions on offer.

What did the series do until now?

Polygon notes that these characters debuted in GTA 3 in 2001. They stood on the street at night and the player could pay them 100 dollars. On screen, only a bouncing car hinted at what happened. The feature became infamous for another reason: many players treated the series as a chaos simulator and killed those NPCs to take the money back, which the outlet describes as a running joke in the community.

The other source of notoriety is the Hot Coffee affair in GTA San Andreas. Rockstar pulled an explicit minigame from the retail release, but it stayed in the files. In 2005, with the PC port, modders found it and released it. Polygon adds that GTA V kept the formula, with even more explicit animations and the first-person view, and that it included a caricature of transgender sex workers that queer players strongly criticised.

Are prostitutes really gone from GTA 6?

Not entirely, Polygon says: given the number of encounters in the game, a sex worker could still show up somewhere. As highly visible NPCs approached freely on the street, however, they are gone. This morning's post from the X account GTAlphaintel points the same way: no paid encounters and no intimacy with random NPCs. That single account is not proof; the Polygon piece comes from an outlet that played the game.

What does not change is the presence of scenes between Jason and Lucia. The same watch accounts mention a relationship that can lead to intimate scenes, but Rockstar has not detailed it. All of it remains to be confirmed by players further into the story.

For context, we already covered other ways the game is more restrained: cheat codes only unlock after the story ends, and the campaign has 119 main missions. Together these choices sketch a more structured GTA.

Why it matters

After twenty-five years, Rockstar removes one of the series' most criticised mechanics, in line with the game's more sincere tone. For players it is one fewer system; the PEGI 18 rating stands. We will see whether other street behaviours change too. More in our journal.
