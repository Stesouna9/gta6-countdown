GTA 6: Leicester shop owner sold just five copies because boxes hold only a code

BBC reports an independent game shop in Leicester sold five copies of GTA 6, as the boxed edition contains a download code rather than a disc.

Sources bbc.com

The biggest game of the decade is not helping every business. BBC reports that Richard Tewksbury, who runs Super Game Shack in Leicester's Silver Arcade, sold only five copies of GTA 6. He calls the launch "embarrassing" and a "write-off" for his shop.

Why so few sales over the counter?

Because the box holds no disc. According to the BBC, Rockstar said in June that physical copies would carry a download code instead of the traditional format. "No one sadly wants to buy a code in a box," he says, adding that many players did not even know the boxed version existed. Many had already pre-ordered digitally months ago because of the download size.

He still loves the game, calling it "amazing" after thirteen years of waiting. The BBC cites Sensor Tower's pre-launch estimate of about £1.25bn in revenue from 17.07m units. The success is real; it just does not pass through his till.

A sign for the whole industry?

He believes this model is the future. The BBC notes that many consoles no longer have disc drives and that PlayStation announced in July its games would stop shipping on disc from January 2028. Rockstar had been contacted for comment.

For players, nothing changes on price: €79.99 for the standard edition, €99.99 for the digital-only Ultimate. See also our release page.

Why it matters

One shop is not a statistic, and the BBC gives no national figure for physical sales. But the Leicester case raises a fair question: when the year's biggest game is a download, what is left for small retailers?
