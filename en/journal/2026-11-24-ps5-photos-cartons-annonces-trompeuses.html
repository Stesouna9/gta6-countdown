GTA 6 demand: misleading PS5 listings offer photos or empty boxes

Le Journal du Geek flags second-hand PS5 listings whose descriptions reveal a photograph or packaging rather than a usable console.

Sources journaldugeek.com

A PS5 pictured in a listing is not always the item being offered. Journal du Geek describes misleading second-hand advertisements circulating around GTA 6's release, including offers where a line near the end of the description changes what the buyer would receive. The overall presentation can suggest usable hardware while the actual offer concerns something else.

What is being offered instead of a console?

The Journal du Geek report identifies photographs and empty packaging, and separately describes requests to move payments away from the marketplace. These are different situations. What connects them is a basic mismatch worth resolving before any purchase: does the offer actually concern the console the reader thinks they are seeing?

The article supplies examples rather than a complete measurement of the problem. It does not establish a victim count, the frequency of such advertisements or their share of all second-hand listings. Its warning can be useful without turning individual cases into a claim about every seller or a statistic about the whole market.

Why does the full description matter?

A photograph shows what is represented. It does not, by itself, define what will be shipped. The category, headline, complete description and sale conditions need to describe the same object. A qualification at the bottom is not a minor detail when it changes a piece of hardware into a printed image.

It also helps to separate enthusiasm for a game from the hardware decision. Wanting to explore Vice City or join a conversation can make an offer feel urgent, but urgency does not explain what the package contains. Our GTA 6 price page concerns the game; those prices do not establish what a separate console advertisement includes.

An unresolved ambiguity is useful information in its own right. A buyer should not have to treat a picture as a promise of a complete working device when the description does not clearly support that interpretation. The offer needs to make sense before its price can meaningfully be compared with another one.

Why it matters

The report shifts attention from the number beside an advertisement to the object behind that number. Comparing prices only works when the goods being compared are equivalent. Packaging, a photograph and functioning hardware are not interchangeable versions of the same purchase, however similar their listing images might look.

This is a reason to pause at precisely the point where an attractive presentation encourages a quick decision. The collected article says Vinted was contacted for more information, but it contains no response from the platform. That limit should remain visible: the report cannot be turned into a platform endorsement, a complete outcome assessment or an assurance that its text does not provide.
