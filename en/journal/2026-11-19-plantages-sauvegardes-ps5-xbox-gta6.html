GTA 6 save issues: PS5 and Xbox players report lost progress after crashes

Notebookcheck describes missing progress after console interruptions, with no confirmed cause or measure of how widespread the issue is.

Sources notebookcheck.net

A console interruption can be frustrating enough without discovering that the last stretch of a game has disappeared too. Notebookcheck reports that some GTA 6 players on PS5 and Xbox have had to repeat missions after crashes or abrupt shutdowns. Its account draws on individual reports, rather than a Rockstar investigation, and does not establish how many players are affected.

What do the early reports actually show?

The most visible example involves streamer JasonTheWeen, who reportedly lost ten hours of progress. Notebookcheck explicitly leaves an important detail unresolved: whether his PS5 crashed or was accidentally switched off. It also notes that some viewers questioned whether the incident was genuine. That makes it an uncertain example, rather than proof of a universal save problem.

The publication also points to Reddit accounts involving Xbox Series X. One player described having to restart the prologue several times; another reported repeated crashes during a session. These experiences warrant attention, but they cannot tell us the failure rate across the audience. The available account does not isolate a common cause or demonstrate that every missing save has the same explanation.

Where should players look before starting again?

According to Notebookcheck, automatic saves appear in the load-game interface rather than the separate save-game menu. Looking in the loading list is therefore a useful check before assuming that all recent progress has vanished. It is not a recovery tool, and it cannot restore a backup that was never written or guarantee that a damaged file will work.

Notebookcheck recommends making manual saves when the game allows it. It says that parts of the prologue and some mission checkpoints may not trigger the automatic recording that players expect. The report supplies no official schedule for those backups. A checkpoint reached during play should therefore not be treated as independent confirmation that a fresh permanent save exists.

Why it matters

The practical priority is to check available files and keep manual progress where possible, rather than assume that the last hour is protected. There is not enough evidence here to blame overloaded hardware, promise a patch or describe a widespread failure. Our release page covers the broader launch, while these save reports remain an issue that needs clearer confirmation and a measurable scope.
