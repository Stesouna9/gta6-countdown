GTA 6 on Xbox Series X: why Rockstar skipped FSR 4, per Digital Foundry

The game renders at 1440p internally with an in-house TAA upscale to 4K on Series X and PS5; Digital Foundry argues FSR 4 costs too much frame time, unconfirmed by Rockstar.

Sources digitalfoundry.net

After the wave of reviews, a question is circulating among Xbox players: why doesn't GTA 6 use the FSR 4 upscaler on Series X? Digital Foundry answers in an analysis published Wednesday, November 18, stating up front that this is conjecture and Rockstar has explained nothing.

How does GTA 6 build its image on Series X?

According to Digital Foundry, the game renders at 1440p internally, then scales to 4K with Rockstar's in-house TAA. On Xbox Series S it renders at 720p for a 1080p output, and there the studio did use FSR 4. The base PS5 follows the Series X pattern: 1440p internal, TAA, 4K output, no Sony QSSR. The PS5 Pro is the odd one out with PSSR, in line with the review complaints about the 30 fps cap.

Why not FSR 4 on Series X?

The outlet relies on figures Microsoft published for Xbox developers earlier this month: 3.86 ms for full FSR 4 and 2.75 ms for the Lite version, both well above the cost of Rockstar's TAA. To absorb 3 to 4 ms, the internal resolution would have to drop. But GTA 6 makes wide use of ray-traced effects, even on Series S, and their quality suffers at lower resolutions. On Series S, FSR 4 costs 2.9 ms (1.9 ms Lite), and the visual gain was apparently judged worth it, even though that console struggles most to hold 30 fps.

The same logic applies to the base PS5: Digital Foundry cites QSSR costing about 4.4 ms more than the in-house solution in Marvel's Wolverine at 4K output.

Why it matters

For anyone playing tomorrow, image quality is set by these technical choices, not by a setting to change. It all remains a hypothesis until Rockstar speaks, as Digital Foundry itself says. Release and platform details are on the release page.
