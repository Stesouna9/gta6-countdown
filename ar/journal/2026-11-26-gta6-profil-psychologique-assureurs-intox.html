GTA 6: no proof Rockstar sells player profiles to insurers, per Boursorama

Boursorama says no concrete evidence backs the claim that Rockstar sells GTA 6 players’ psychological profiles to insurers: it comes from an anonymous account.

مصادر boursorama.com

No, nothing shows that Rockstar sells GTA 6 players’ psychological profiles to insurers. That is the conclusion of a fact-check video from Boursorama published overnight on November 25-26: the claim comes from an anonymous account, and “no concrete evidence allows anyone to say so”.

What is Rockstar accused of?

According to Boursorama, GTA 6’s launch is described by its publisher as the biggest in video game history. On social media, that success comes with a wave of false claims. One of them, from an anonymous account, accuses Rockstar of selling players’ psychological profiles to insurance companies. The video, framed as an “info or hoax” check, files the claim under hoax. It gives no technical or legal detail here: the finding is the absence of proof.

Where does the mix-up with the criminal profile come from?

The game does have a “criminal profile”, but it is a gameplay mechanic. Rob Nelson, head of development at Rockstar North, described it to Eurogamer as a way to give weight to the player’s actions: the system judges how harsh you are, not the person holding the controller. We covered that explanation in our piece on the criminal profile. Nothing Rockstar has said publicly links it to personal data or insurers.

The word “profile” invites confusion, and that is the kind of slide these posts feed on: a game term, a real worry about data, and a message tying the two together without proof.

What do we know, and what is still unknown?

Established: the claim is circulating, its author is anonymous, and Boursorama found nothing to back it. Not established: where the message came from, how far it spread, and any reaction from Rockstar or Take-Two, which we have not found so far. The video also points to a wider online trend, the “fake laid-off employee email”, a format that claims to reveal company secrets from the inside.

For players worried about their data, the sensible move is to read Rockstar’s and Take-Two’s privacy policies and to distrust any revelation that cites no document, no name and no verifiable source. We follow the story in the Buzz section.

Why it matters

Little for the game itself, but it is a reminder that a launch this size attracts false claims as much as real ones. Until proof or an official answer appears, the insurer story should be read as an unfounded rumor, not as fact.
