GTA 6 in China: unverified guide tells Douyin streamers not to steal cars

Newsweek reports an unverified guide circulating for Douyin streamers; the game has no approval in mainland China, where Rockstar nonetheless opened an account.

출처 newsweek.com

GTA 6 has no approval for sale in mainland China, and streaming it already raises questions. Newsweek reports that an unverified document titled "Guidelines for Compliance During Livestreams of GTA 6" is circulating on Chinese blogs and sites such as Gamersky and 3DM Game.

What does the document tell streamers?

Per Newsweek, it would ask streamers on Douyin, China's TikTok, not to steal cars, attack civilians or fight police, and to avoid red blood and revealing outfits. Newsweek says it could not verify it: it emailed Douyin and Rockstar and is awaiting replies. The guidance would fit the platform's existing rules against bloody imagery and violent games.

Can people play it in China?

Yes, through the gray market: the franchise never received official approval, and players buy the game on other markets such as Hong Kong, which authorities generally ignore, according to Newsweek. The 2022 game-streaming rules do ask platforms not to stream unapproved games, hence tighter limits on live broadcasts.

Rockstar has nonetheless opened a verified Douyin account with over 480,000 followers. Its only post urges players to preload before the November 19 launch on PS5 and Xbox Series X|S. The game was also censored beforehand: the 27-minute preview shown on Netflix in August was heavily blurred on Bilibili, hiding the nightclub, dancing, smoking and swimwear.
