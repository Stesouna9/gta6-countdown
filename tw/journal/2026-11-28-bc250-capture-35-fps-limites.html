GTA 6 on AMD BC-250: a 35 FPS screenshot is no PS5 benchmark

Notebookcheck reports a modified BC-250 running the game. A single screenshot cannot establish sustained performance or superiority over PS5.

圖片：Rockstar Games

來源 notebookcheck.biz

A modified AMD BC-250 reportedly ran a GTA 6 scene at 35 frames per second, according to Notebookcheck.fr. The evidence described is a screenshot showing Lucia in prison, rather than a sustained benchmark. That makes this an unusual hardware experiment worth examining, but it does not establish that the board outperforms a standard PS5 during normal play or across the wider game.

Why is this mining board relevant?

Notebookcheck explains that the BC-250 uses a reduced version of the PS5's processor design, sharing related CPU and GPU architectures and GDDR6 memory. The boards were intended for cryptocurrency mining before being repurposed for other applications. That connection helps explain the experiment's appeal, without making an unmodified board equivalent to a ready-to-use PlayStation console.

The publication discusses modifications that can release additional performance. It describes this particular example as apparently unlocked or overclocked. The collected account does not establish exactly which tools were used. Consequently, the report cannot be turned into a universal setup guide or evidence of an ordinary, supported way to play the game on a computer.

What does the displayed frame rate establish?

Notebookcheck attributes the result to an internet user named djaye. It says the screenshot shows 35 FPS with lows of 29. A prison scene provides little evidence about a long drive, dense crowds or the consistency of the experience over an extended session. It is a narrow glimpse of a running scene, with important conditions still undocumented.

The publication acknowledges that one screenshot cannot compare real-time performance against a standard PS5. That qualification is more significant than the headline's suggestion of superior results. A momentary reading does not establish an average, smooth delivery or a lasting advantage. Any meaningful comparison would also need equivalent image quality and settings rather than frame rate numbers considered in isolation.

Why it matters

The new element is an unexpected use of repurposed hardware, rather than a demonstrated replacement for Sony's console. The source does not establish that the modifications are publicly available or that an independent party has verified a complete play session. Its optimistic conclusion should therefore be read alongside the limitations it also describes.

This joins other performance videos whose numbers require context. It is not an announcement of an official Rockstar PC release. In our GTA 6 technical coverage, the useful next step would be extended footage with documented conditions and a comparable console run. Until then, the screenshot is an intriguing lead for hardware enthusiasts, not sufficient evidence to recommend replacing a PS5 with a mining board.
