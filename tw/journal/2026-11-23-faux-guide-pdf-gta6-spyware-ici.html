GTA 6 free PC download: fake PDF guide delivers spyware, ICI reports

ICI reports an Avast warning about a fake GTA 6 download guide that leads to spyware. There is no announced official PC edition.

圖片：Rockstar Games

來源 ici.fr

A free GTA 6 download can come with a very different payload. French broadcaster ICI describes a fraudulent PDF download guide in its November 23 report, citing an Avast warning about spyware. The offer uses interest in the game to draw people towards software that can collect personal information rather than provide the promised game.

What makes the PDF look convincing?

According to ICI, the guide has a clean, professional layout with a prominent download button. It even includes a notice telling readers that they are leaving for a third-party website. In this context, an apparently cautious message can help make a suspicious offer feel routine. It does not establish who supplied the software or whether that software is safe.

The broadcaster reports that Avast identified spyware behind the offer. Login details and passwords are among the personal data it says can be taken. That is the mechanism described in the report, rather than a measured count of affected players. ICI does not provide a victim total for this particular PDF or a tally of accounts compromised through it.

The PC claim is another warning sign

The download guide also claims to offer a computer version, ICI notes. No official PC edition of GTA 6 has been announced. A page presenting a PC download as an official release therefore conflicts with the known platform situation. Our earlier warning about fake installers addressed emulator claims; this report describes a specific PDF lure. Reports about unofficial projects do not amount to a Rockstar release or establish the authenticity of a downloadable file.

There is a related distinction in our earlier coverage of the suspicious GTA 6 Steam listing: familiar branding on a page does not prove that the product is genuine. Our prices and editions page provides context for the known offers, which a promise of free access should not erase.

Why it matters

This report gives the download debate a concrete consequence beyond getting a file that fails to run. The spyware described by ICI targets personal account information. Checking where an offer comes from matters more than how polished its instructions appear. The broadcaster recommends official stores as the safest route to obtaining the game. A game logo, a tidy PDF and a reassuring warning cannot establish that an unknown download is legitimate. Those presentation choices are precisely why this example deserves attention alongside the legitimate launch coverage.
