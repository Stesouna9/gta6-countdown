GTA 6 masks: an early shop location and the limits of hiding your face

VICE points to a Key Lento face covering and explains why recognised clothes or a vehicle can still matter.

圖片：Rockstar Games

來源 vice.com

A face covering is only one part of escaping recognition in GTA 6. VICE places an early purchase at Chuck’s Tackle & Shot in Key Lento, close to Jason’s home. Its guide makes a more useful point than simply naming a shop: police recognition can concern a face, an outfit and a vehicle separately.

Where does the guide send players?

According to VICE, the shop’s Neckwear section includes the Reel Neck Gaiter for 35 in-game dollars during Chapter 1. An alligator head above the display serves as a visual landmark. That gives players a specific place to begin looking, rather than a vague suggestion to visit any clothing store.

VICE also separates ordinary face coverings from the mask Raul offers ahead of a Chapter 2 robbery. An item seen in a scripted sequence should not automatically be treated as standard shop stock. This report is about the starting point described in that guide, rather than an independently checked inventory of every available mask.

Why might changing your face be insufficient?

VICE says removing a mask after breaking line of sight can help clear appearance recognition. However, recognised clothing or a getaway vehicle can retain their own Heat indicators beneath the wanted stars. In that account, resolving one source of identification does not necessarily resolve the others.

For a reader, the distinction is between owning equipment and understanding what the interface is reporting. A successful purchase establishes neither the right moment to use it nor the outcome of a later chase. Looking at the remaining indicators provides a way to interpret an unsuccessful escape without immediately assuming that the whole system has failed.

Why it matters

The practical value lies in the relationship between those clues. A face covering, a different outfit and another car should be considered as separate pieces of information, rather than interchangeable guarantees. Our community page offers a place to discuss experiences, while this article keeps its claim tied to the behaviour VICE describes.

That boundary matters for a guide published after launch. It supplies a shop location and an explanation of visible feedback, but does not promise that one action will end every pursuit. Readers can use it as a starting point while keeping the circumstances of their own session in view.
