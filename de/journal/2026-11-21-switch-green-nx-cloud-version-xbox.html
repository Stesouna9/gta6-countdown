GTA 6 on Switch: green-nx streams Xbox gameplay, HardwareCooking reports

An unofficial cloud client reportedly brings an Xbox video stream to a modified original Switch. It is not a native Nintendo release.

Foto: Rockstar Games

Quellen hardwarecooking.fr

A report of GTA 6 running on Nintendo’s original Switch needs one crucial qualification: the console is receiving a cloud stream. HardwareCooking describes green-nx as an unofficial Xbox Cloud Gaming client for a modified Switch. The account does not establish a native Nintendo port, an eShop release or an announcement from Rockstar about another supported console.

What is the Switch actually doing?

In the setup described by HardwareCooking, Xbox hardware in the cloud runs the game. The Switch decodes incoming video and sends controller input back to the service. Displaying that feed is a different technical achievement from adapting GTA 6’s engine to execute on Nintendo’s handheld. The report should not be used as proof of the latter.

The publication credits developer rmrf404 with the open-source project and says it requires an original Switch running Atmosphère. This is a modified software environment, rather than a Nintendo store download. HardwareCooking also states that the project has no affiliation with Microsoft or Nintendo, an important detail when assessing what the demonstration represents.

What would access require?

According to the report, users need to own a digital Xbox copy and have the appropriate cloud access. The game is a separate purchase. We are not reproducing its monthly allowance figures or subscription-tier claims here, because the material consulted does not independently establish them through Microsoft documentation. Anyone considering spending money should check the service conditions applying to their own account first.

The client’s claimed ability to handle a 60-frame-per-second stream also needs context. HardwareCooking explicitly distinguishes the video feed from the frame rate of the game being streamed. A client receiving frames at that rate does not turn a 30 FPS game into a 60 FPS release. Our coverage of Rockstar’s explanation of console frame-rate limits addresses that separate issue.

Why it matters

HardwareCooking acknowledges that no latency measurements or benchmarks have been published in the material it describes. Its account therefore cannot guarantee responsive driving or combat. It also identifies the original Switch as the target hardware, with no announced Switch 2 adaptation. Those limits matter more than a headline suggesting a new console edition.

For now, this is an interesting report about streaming software, without an independent client test from us or confirmation from the platform holders. It leaves the official platforms on our release page unchanged and supplies no evidence of a PC port. Our PC release coverage explains what is known about that different question.
