GTA 6: UK schools fear pupil absences on launch week, says the BBC

Essex schools are offering Amazon vouchers and Christmas dinners to stop pupils skipping class for GTA 6, the BBC reports.

Quellen bbc.com

The release of GTA 6 is pushing British secondary schools to react. According to the BBC, an Essex school is running a raffle for £10 Amazon vouchers and free Christmas dinners to keep pupils in class.

What is Sandon School in Chelmsford offering?

The game dropped in the UK at midnight. To avoid empty classrooms for the rest of the week, Sandon School also offers a lunchtime queue-jump pass for the rest of the year. A letter to parents says attendance "remains a priority" and that absence to play, buy or engage with the game during school hours will be recorded as unauthorised. Head teacher Jon Pead says he does not expect half-empty classrooms on Thursday and Friday but wants to be proactive.

The letter adds the game "is expected to carry an 18+ rating" and is not designed for children. The game is rated PEGI 18.

Are other schools doing the same?

Not all alike, per the BBC. Ramsey Academy in Halstead, Essex, rewards pupils with sweet treats and house points. In St Neots, Longsands Academy told parents it expects all students to attend as normal. In Woodbridge, Suffolk, Farlingaye High School reminded parents of £80 penalties for persistent unauthorised absence, even for tiredness after a late night.

Why it matters

The stakes go beyond the game: secondary attendance fell from about 94% to 91% since Covid, the BBC notes. Chris Janes of the University of Suffolk calls it a cultural touchstone: games are no longer niche, "they're like Hollywood films now". See also the midnight launch in France and the release page.
