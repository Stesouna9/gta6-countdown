GTA 6: over 1,600 Rockstar staff asked to vote on union recognition

British Rockstar workers are voting on a recognition agreement, with the consultation due to close on November 26, BFM reports.

Foto: Rockstar Games

More than 1,600 British Rockstar employees have been asked to vote on union recognition, according to BFM. The ballot opened on Thursday, November 12, and is due to remain open until November 26. That places the consultation on both sides of GTA 6’s November 19 launch, with no result yet available.

What would the agreement recognise?

BFM identifies the organisation as the Rockstar Game Workers Union, part of the Independent Workers’ Union of Great Britain. The union says the agreement follows four months of negotiations between management and employees. Workers are being asked about workplace representation, rather than to approve or reject the release of the game.

According to the union’s account carried by BFM, recognition would create a framework for negotiating pay, overtime, bonuses, holidays and pensions. The proposed arrangement also includes consultation rights on human resources policies and artificial intelligence. These are descriptions of what the agreement would provide if approved, not benefits whose implementation can already be taken for granted.

How does the dismissal dispute fit in?

The report also describes the Scottish legal proceedings concerning 23 dismissals in October 2025. The claimants allege that their union activity was the reason they lost their jobs. Rockstar argues that confidential information was shared in insecure Discord discussion channels. Those positions need to remain clearly attributed while the proceedings continue.

A recognition agreement and a case about past dismissals address different questions. Progress on one should not be reported as a court ruling on the other. Their proximity to the launch makes the timing striking, but does not establish that either process has already reached its conclusion.

Why it matters

The approaching release is a milestone for the workforce as well as for customers. A successful recognition vote could establish an ongoing structure for discussing how Rockstar’s British employees are paid and represented. The IWGB presents the potential agreement as significant for the wider games industry, which is the union’s assessment rather than an independently settled outcome.

For readers following our launch calendar, BFM’s report contains no announcement of a delay or a distribution problem. The next concrete development is the ballot result and, if workers approve the text, the arrangements that follow. Reporting the vote accurately means taking its possible consequences seriously while leaving the undecided questions open.
