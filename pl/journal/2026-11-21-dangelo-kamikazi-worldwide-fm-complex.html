GTA 6 radio features unreleased D’Angelo track, Complex reports

Complex says “Kamikazi” plays on Worldwide FM, citing an authenticity confirmation from DJ Dojah reported by The Washington Post.

Fot. Rockstar Games

Źródła mashable.com

An unreleased D’Angelo recording called “Kamikazi” is playing on GTA 6’s Worldwide FM, according to Mashable. The outlet says longtime collaborator DJ Dojah confirmed the recording’s authenticity to The Washington Post. Our reporting is based on Complex’s collected article text; its original page was unavailable through our web tool, and we have not independently examined that confirmation.

What is the new discovery on Worldwide FM?

Complex identifies two separate D’Angelo appearances in the game. “Kamikazi” is the previously unheard recording on Worldwide FM, while the established song “Lady” appears on Honey FM. The latter dates to 1995, the outlet notes. Keeping those details separate matters: hearing an artist on a station does not automatically mean every track being played is new.

The collected account does not establish when “Kamikazi” was recorded, who selected it for the station, or whether a standalone release is planned. None of those gaps should be filled by inference. A recording becoming accessible within a game is a different event from a single arriving on streaming services. The report supports the former claim, with the attribution above, rather than a broader release announcement.

Rockstar and D’Angelo already had a musical connection

Complex also points to “Unshaken,” which D’Angelo contributed to Red Dead Redemption 2. That earlier collaboration helps explain why this discovery has attracted attention from Rockstar listeners as well as soul fans. It does not establish a shared recording session or a common production history for the two songs. The article provides no such link.

Przeczytaj też Tajne stacje radiowe GTA 6 : jak je odblokować za pomocą skanów plakatów

Our GTA 6 music page follows the wider soundtrack. The reported Spotify radio playlists and the official album release are separate stories. Their availability should not be treated as proof that every piece of music heard in Leonida has a matching release outside the game. For this recording, the platform claim remains limited to the station identified by Complex.

Why it matters

Game radio can provide more than familiar songs for a journey. In this case, according to Complex, it offers access to an unheard recording by an artist already connected to Rockstar’s work. That is the meaningful development. Questions about release rights, recording dates and future distribution remain open in the material we read. Until a direct announcement from Rockstar or the relevant rights holders is examined, the discovery should retain its source attribution rather than become an unconditional claim about a new commercial single.
