GTA 6 studio union recognition vote rejected, according to Kotaku

Kotaku reports that 75% voted against recognition of the IWGB in the UK Rockstar ballot.

Fot. Rockstar Games

Źródła kotaku.com

A UK Rockstar workforce ballot has rejected a recognition agreement with the IWGB Game Workers Union, according to Kotaku. The outlet reports that 75% voted against the proposal. This is a result about formal collective representation at the GTA 6 developer, rather than a decision that prevents individual employees from belonging to a union.

What was the recognition ballot about?

Kotaku says voting began on November 12 and ended on November 26. Recognition would have formalised collective bargaining on issues including overtime, bonuses, redundancies and artificial intelligence. The proposed arrangement concerned an established channel between the union and management for discussing employment policy, rather than a vote on a particular feature or decision inside GTA 6.

Employees who support organising can still work with the IWGB, the report explains. That continuing relationship should be distinguished from the recognition mechanism that failed. The reported percentage also describes this ballot; it cannot be used to claim that every Rockstar employee across the company's international studios holds the same position.

The IWGB says its campaign will continue

In a statement carried by Kotaku, the union says it intends to keep building understanding and trust among colleagues. It identifies pay transparency, contractual overtime pay and protections concerning AI and layoffs as priorities. Those are the union's stated aims, not evidence that management has now agreed to implement the policies.

Kotaku also places the ballot against an ongoing dispute over previously dismissed workers. Those workers accused Rockstar of union busting, an allegation the company denies. The outlet reports that a labour tribunal case remains unresolved, with a final judgment still pending. The outcome of the recognition ballot does not settle those allegations or replace the tribunal's eventual decision.

Why it matters

The immediate reported consequence is that this recognition agreement will not take effect. It does not establish a permanent end to organising efforts, and it does not announce any change to GTA 6 itself. Release plans, updates and game content cannot be inferred from the ballot. Our GTA 6 journal also follows the production context behind the game.

Two developments now need to be followed separately: whether the union can broaden support after this defeat, and what happens in the legal dispute concerning the dismissed workers. Treating the vote as a judgment on the court case would collapse different processes into one. The information reported by Kotaku supports a distinction between them, not a conclusion that either has resolved the other.
