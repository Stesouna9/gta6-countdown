Rockstar open to a Red Dead Redemption adaptation, says The Ankler

The Ankler claims Rockstar is more open to adapting Red Dead Redemption for film or TV while still refusing GTA. Rockstar has confirmed nothing.

Źródła theankler.com

Rockstar has never given in to Hollywood's calls to adapt Grand Theft Auto. But another of its franchises may be more accessible, claims The Ankler in a scoop by Lesley Goldberg based on several anonymous sources. Rockstar had not responded to the outlet's request for comment at publication.

What exactly does the scoop say?

According to The Ankler, Rockstar, a Take-Two subsidiary, "doesn't need Hollywood" and stays closed to a GTA adaptation despite years of calls from entertainment companies. By contrast, several sources say the publisher would be more open to bringing Red Dead Redemption to film or television.

How much would Rockstar ask?

An agent working with video game writers says "at one point, they were throwing around a purchase price of more than $100 million minimum" for the rights. The outlet compares that to the $250 million Amazon paid in 2017 for The Lord of the Rings TV rights. This is a reported remark from a single source, not a confirmed figure.

Why now?

The Ankler ties the report to GTA 6's launch, which it expected to generate $3 billion to $5 billion in its first week. Rockstar itself announced $3.79 billion in day-one sales, as covered on the price page and in our piece on the scale of the launch. The outlet also notes Amazon, Netflix and FX are spending $15 million to $25 million per episode on Tomb Raider, Assassin's Creed and Far Cry adaptations.

Why it matters

Nothing official yet: neither Rockstar nor Take-Two has confirmed talks. If the scoop holds, it would not involve GTA, which the publisher reportedly still refuses to adapt. We will follow any Take-Two reaction. Meanwhile, Rockstar news lives on the journal.
